Apple Silicon Testing

TL;DR — M6 vs M4 Pro for AI workloads: neither chip dominates; it splits by phase.
  • Ingesting prompts (prefill / time-to-first-token): the M6 is ~2–3× faster (+120% to +212% across all three models and prompt lengths 512→8192; roughly 3–4× per GPU core). This is the M6's new GPU tensor units at work.
  • Generating tokens (decode): the M4 Pro is ~1.4–1.6× faster (the M6 is 28–37% slower on every model and depth). Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound and the M4 Pro has more of it.
  • Mixed chat roughly cancels — short-prompt conversations lean M4 Pro, long-context work leans M6. Prompt-heavy pipelines (RAG, long documents, coding context) are the M6's clear win; long generations are the M4 Pro's.
  • The Neural Engine is not the shortcut (at small-model scale): 3–7× slower than each chip's own GPU at decode, and on the measured device (M4 Pro) less efficient (6.8–7.7 tok/J vs the M6 GPU's 19.9 tok/J). Its real trick: GPU power drops to ~zero during ANE inference.
  • Memory: gpt-oss-20b (12.1 GB peak) runs on both laptops; Qwen3-30B-A3B needs the 64 GB Mac Studio.

A benchmark side quest measuring local LLM inference on Apple Silicon: single-stream prompt throughput (prefill / TTFT), decode tok/s, peak memory, and power efficiency across three inference paths — MLX (GPU), llama.cpp (Metal cross-check), and the Neural Engine via ANEMLL/Core ML. The single source of truth, with every raw number and method note, is HiQS-AI-Resolve-Catalog #5; this page is the readable overview. Last updated 2026-09-30.

Q1 — GPU neural accelerators (prefill)

The M6 is the only tested chip whose GPU has the new tensor units (MLX ships 6,322 _nax kernels in its wheel on the M6's macOS 27 vs zero on the M4 Pro's macOS 15; llama.cpp reports has tensor = true only on the M6). With both laptops at full power, MLX prefill on the M6 is:

ModelM6 vs M4 Pro prefillDecode (M4 Pro faster)
Qwen3-1.7B+185% → +212% (p512 → p8192)M6 −28% → −30%
Qwen3-8B+190% → +212%M6 −37% → −34%
gpt-oss-20b+120% → +180%M6 −35% → −29%

So: prefill (compute-bound) is 2.2–3.1× faster on the M6 — roughly 2.9–4.2× per GPU core with 12 cores against 16 — while decode (bandwidth-bound) is 1.3–1.6× faster on the M4 Pro. The first (superseded) comparison measured the M4 Pro in Low Power Mode and overstated the prefill gap at 4.1–4.8× and hid the decode gap entirely; the corrected comparison is the one above.

Q2 — decode tok/s

Decode is memory-bandwidth-bound, and here the ranking inverts: the M4 Pro decodes 28–37% faster on every model and depth. Neither chip dominates end to end — long-prompt interactive workloads trade M6 TTFT against M4 Pro decode. A 10-minute sustained run on the M6 (Qwen3-8B, 16,978 tokens, 28.29 tok/s average, sampling every 30 s) showed no thermal throttling, validating the short-benchmark numbers.

Q3 — the Neural Engine (ANE)

The ANEMLL Qwen3-1.7B Core ML build was measured on both laptops:

MetricM6 (macOS 27)M4 Pro (macOS 15.6)
ANE decode44.1–44.4 tok/s26.6 tok/s
ANE prefill @ 516 tok1605.8 tok/s942.7 tok/s
ANE ÷ own-MLX decode0.33×0.14×
ANE power (rail)unmeasurable — macOS 27 hides the rail~2.1 W (GPU below idle during inference)
EfficiencyMLX GPU reference: 19.9 tok/J6.8–7.7 tok/J (ANE path)
ane_verifiedn (rail hidden by OS)y — campaign first

Findings: ANE placement is verified three independent ways on the M6 (static MLComputePlan — 99–100% of ops on ANE; GPU rail at idle during ANE inference; and CPU_AND_NE ≡ ALL at runtime while CPU_AND_GPU cannot even compile the LUT matmul for Metal). The M6 ANE is a flat 1.66–1.70× the M4 Pro's on every metric. The M4 Pro's whole-session ANE efficiency (6.8–7.7 tok/J) is below the M6 GPU's 19.9 tok/J — at S-tier the ANE is not the efficiency win; its real trick is dropping GPU power to zero. And the M6 shows a unique bimodal speed state (fast/slow runs, 29/11 of 40) that did not reproduce on the M4 Pro — an M6-side anomaly, still open.
